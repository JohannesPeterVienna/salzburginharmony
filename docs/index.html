How a walk works

  1. Read it first. Every walk has an introduction, a box with checked opening times and prices, a sketch of the route, and six to eight stops with the full text visible without any script.
  2. Choose a host. Alois tells you about the archbishops, the walls and the view; Theresa about the gardens, the food and what people do here in each season. Both are invented characters with real knowledge of the city.
  3. Press start. Walk mode shows one stop at a time with a progress bar, the host's remark and a small observation task. It remembers where you stopped. Print it or save the plan as text.
  4. Plan the day. The day planner adds up your minutes at the stops and gives you a schedule to copy or print. It does not calculate walking times or queues, and it says so.

Why these walks

Salzburg is small, baroque and built around a river and three hills, which makes it a walker's city. Most visitors see the same squares on a tight schedule and miss the convent above the roofs, the walls on the hill across the water, the pond south of town and the market where locals buy their cheese. These walks put the famous places and the quiet ones in the same order a Salzburger would choose.

Everything factual on this site is checked against the official sources named at the bottom of each walk, with the date we looked. Where we could not verify a price or an opening time, we say so and point you to the operator. Where a place is private, like Leopoldskron, we tell you to stay outside. Start with Mirabell to the Fortress for the classic line, add the Kapuzinerberg for the view most people miss, and keep Hellbrunn for a half day. Coming in December? Winter Salzburg lists what is open and what is not.
